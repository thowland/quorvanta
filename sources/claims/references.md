**REF-002** Halvorsen KE, Mbatha-Reid L, Ossowski P, Trenholm A, Okafor DN, for the BRIGHTWATER Study Group. Placebo-controlled trial of dimethyl tavorate in relapsing-remitting Brennick syndrome (BRIGHTWATER). *Brennick Syndrome Research and Treatment* 2015;32(4):611-623. doi:10.5555/bsrt.2015.32.611  
Type: journal_article.

- `REF-002.1` Of 1,180 randomized patients, 394 received DMT 200 mg twice daily, 392 three times daily and 394 placebo.
- `REF-002.2` At 2 years, 25% of patients on DMT twice daily had relapsed versus 43% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.54; 46% relative risk reduction; p<0.0001).
- `REF-002.3` The annualized relapse rate was 0.18 with DMT twice daily and 0.35 with placebo (49% relative reduction; p<0.0001).
- `REF-002.4` Confirmed disability progression at 12 weeks occurred in 15% of DMT twice-daily patients and 25% of placebo patients (41% relative risk reduction; p=0.003).
- `REF-002.5` In the MRI cohort (160 DMT, 158 placebo), mean new or newly enlarging T2 lesions over 2 years were 3.1 versus 15.8; mean contrast-enhancing lesions at 2 years were 0.2 versus 1.6 (p<0.0001 for both).
- `REF-002.6` Three-times-daily dosing offered no additional benefit over twice-daily dosing on any endpoint.
- `REF-002.7` Flushing (34% versus 5%) and gastrointestinal events were the most frequent adverse reactions and were most common in the first two months.

**REF-003** Sørlie-Nakamura T, Achterberg RJ, Villaseñor M, Pryce-Dunn H, Halvorsen KE, for the CASTLEREAGH Study Group. Dimethyl tavorate versus placebo in relapsing-remitting Brennick syndrome, with an open-label reference arm (CASTLEREAGH). *Brennick Syndrome Research and Treatment* 2015;32(4):624-637. doi:10.5555/bsrt.2015.32.624  
Type: journal_article. Note: Finding REF-003.7 is a trap: it exists in the reference but is not in the label and supports no approved claim.

- `REF-003.1` Of 1,390 randomized patients, 352 received DMT 200 mg twice daily, 348 three times daily, 340 an open-label reference therapy and 350 placebo.
- `REF-003.2` The annualized relapse rate, the primary endpoint, was 0.21 with DMT twice daily and 0.39 with placebo (46% relative reduction; p<0.0001).
- `REF-003.3` The proportion relapsing at 2 years was 28% versus 40% (33% relative risk reduction; p=0.003).
- `REF-003.4` Confirmed disability progression at 12 weeks occurred in 14% versus 18% of patients; the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.21).
- `REF-003.5` In the MRI cohort (150 DMT, 146 placebo), mean new or newly enlarging T2 lesions were 4.8 versus 16.2 and mean contrast-enhancing lesions 0.4 versus 1.9 (p<0.0001 for both).
- `REF-003.6` The reference arm was open-label and the study was not powered for comparison with it; no formal comparison is reported.
- `REF-003.7` In a post hoc analysis, the reference arm had an annualized relapse rate of 0.29, which the authors describe as descriptive only.

**REF-004** Okafor DN, Bergstrom-Lahti E, Sørlie-Nakamura T, Mbatha-Reid L. Bioequivalence of soriximel tavorate and dimethyl tavorate in patients with relapsing Brennick syndrome and in healthy subjects. *Journal of Tavorate Pharmacology* 2019;11(2):88-97. doi:10.5555/jtp.2019.11.88  
Type: journal_article.

- `REF-004.1` Soriximel tavorate 380 mg and dimethyl tavorate 200 mg produced bioequivalent MMT exposure (AUC ratio 0.98, 90% CI 0.93 to 1.03; Cmax ratio 1.02, 90% CI 0.95 to 1.09).
- `REF-004.2` Soriximel tavorate was not quantifiable in plasma; all analyses used MMT concentrations.
- `REF-004.3` Median time to peak MMT concentration was 2 to 2.5 hours; mean Cmax after 380 mg in patients was 1.84 mg/L and mean steady-state AUC 7.1 mg·hr/L.
- `REF-004.4` The terminal half-life of MMT was approximately 1.5 hours with no accumulation on twice-daily dosing.

**REF-005** Bergstrom-Lahti E, Quiroga-Pell S, Okafor DN. Effect of meal composition on monomethyl tavorate pharmacokinetics after soriximel tavorate. *Journal of Tavorate Pharmacology* 2019;11(3):140-148. doi:10.5555/jtp.2019.11.140

**REF-007** Ferrante-Wu J, Okafor DN, Lindqvist-Amara P. Pharmacokinetics of soriximel tavorate and its metabolite hydroxypropyl glavorimide in renal impairment. *Journal of Tavorate Pharmacology* 2020;12(4):201-209. doi:10.5555/jtp.2020.12.201

**REF-008** Villaseñor M, Trenholm A, Pryce-Dunn H. Lymphocyte count dynamics during and after dimethyl tavorate treatment: pooled analysis of controlled and uncontrolled studies. *Archives of Relapsing Neuroinflammatory Disease* 2018;47(6):1102-1114. doi:10.5555/arnd.2018.47.1102

- `REF-008.2` Lymphocyte counts below 0.5 × 10⁹/L occurred in 5% of DMT-treated patients and fewer than 1% of placebo patients.

**REF-024** Tavistock-Moreau E, Lindqvist-Amara P, Okafor DN, Sørlie-Nakamura T for the IBSC Diagnostic Criteria Panel. Diagnosis and classification of Brennick syndrome: 2021 revision of the IBSC criteria. *Brennick Syndrome Research and Treatment* 2021;38(2):141-158. doi:10.5555/bsrt.2021.38.141  
Type: journal_article.

- `REF-024.1` Brennick syndrome is a chronic, immune-mediated disorder of the central nervous system characterized by inflammation around small blood vessels in the brainstem and spinal cord, with damage to nearby nerve fibers.
- `REF-024.2` The disease course is classified as first clinical episode, relapsing-remitting, progressive with activity (active progressive), progressive without activity (non-active progressive), or primary progressive.
- `REF-024.3` A relapse is new or worsening neurological symptoms lasting at least 48 hours, in the absence of fever or infection, at least 30 days after the start of any previous relapse.
- `REF-024.4` Diagnosis requires a typical clinical episode, MRI lesions in at least two of four characteristic regions (brainstem, cervical spinal cord, cerebellar peduncles, periaqueductal region), and exclusion of other causes; a positive cerebrospinal fluid BRK index may substitute for one MRI region.
- `REF-024.5` T2 lesions reflect areas of current or past inflammation; contrast-enhancing (CE+) lesions indicate inflammation active within approximately the previous 6 weeks.

**REF-025** Halvorsen KE, Pryce-Dunn H, Villaseñor M. The Brennick Functional Scale: development, reliability and validity. *Journal of Relapsing Neuroinflammatory Disease* 2011;19(3):201-214. doi:10.5555/jrnd.2011.19.201  
Type: journal_article.

- `REF-025.1` The Brennick Functional Scale (BFS) is a clinician-rated scale from 0 (normal neurological examination) to 8 (unable to walk), in whole-point steps, based on examination of six functional domains (motor, sensory, balance, vision, bladder and bowel, cognition) and walking ability.
- `REF-025.2` BFS 4 corresponds to walking limited to about 500 meters without aid or rest; BFS 6 to needing a walking aid to walk 100 meters.
- `REF-025.3` A change of at least 1 point sustained for 12 weeks exceeded measurement variability and is proposed as the definition of confirmed disability progression.

**REF-028** International Brennick Syndrome Consortium Treatment Guideline Group. IBSC guideline on the treatment of relapsing Brennick syndrome, 2024 update. *Brennick Syndrome Research and Treatment* 2024;41(1):1-29. doi:10.5555/bsrt.2024.41.1
