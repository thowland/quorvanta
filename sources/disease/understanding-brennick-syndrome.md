## What is Brennick syndrome?

Brennick syndrome is a long-term condition in which the immune system causes inflammation in the brainstem and spinal cord, which can damage nerve fibers. `[DIS-001]`

About 410,000 adults in the United States live with Brennick syndrome. Brennick syndrome is more common in women, and it usually starts between the ages of 20 and 45. `[DIS-002, DIS-003]`

Brennick syndrome isn't passed down in a simple way, although having a close relative with it slightly raises the chance of getting it. `[DIS-014]`

## How does it change over time?

Doctors describe Brennick syndrome by how it behaves over time: a first episode, relapsing-remitting, or one of several progressive forms. Most people, about 8 in 10, start with a relapsing form of Brennick syndrome. `[DIS-004, DIS-005]`

A relapse is when new symptoms appear, or old ones get worse, for at least 2 days, without a fever or infection to explain them. `[DIS-006]`

Tiredness (fatigue) is the most common symptom, reported by about 7 in 10 people with Brennick syndrome. `[DIS-013]`

## How is it diagnosed and measured?

Brennick syndrome is diagnosed using your symptoms, MRI scans, and sometimes a test of the fluid around the spinal cord, after other causes have been ruled out. `[DIS-008]`

MRI scans show areas of inflammation. Some show recent activity and some show older changes. `[DIS-009]`

Doctors use a scale called the Brennick Functional Scale, from 0 to 8, to measure how Brennick syndrome affects things like movement, balance, vision and walking. `[DIS-010]`

## Can it be treated?

There are several kinds of long-term treatment for relapsing Brennick syndrome, including pills, injections and infusions. Your healthcare provider can talk with you about which may suit you. `[DIS-015]`
