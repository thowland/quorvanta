## 1 INDICATIONS AND USAGE

QUORVANTA is indicated for the treatment of relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome (BrS), to include first clinical episode, relapsing-remitting disease, and active progressive disease, in adults.

## 2 DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION

### 2.1 Tests prior to initiation

Obtain the following before starting QUORVANTA:

- A complete blood count (CBC), including lymphocyte count (see 5.4)
- Serum aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase and total bilirubin levels (see 5.5)
- An estimate of renal function (see 2.5)

### 2.2 Dosing information

The starting dosage is 190 mg twice a day orally. After 14 days, the dosage should be increased to the maintenance dosage of 380 mg (administered as two 190 mg capsules) twice a day orally.

Temporary dosage reduction to 190 mg twice a day may be considered for patients who do not tolerate the maintenance dosage. The maintenance dosage should be resumed within 6 weeks. Discontinuation should be considered for patients unable to tolerate a return to the maintenance dosage.

Administration of non-enteric coated aspirin (up to 325 mg) 30 minutes before dosing may reduce the incidence or severity of flushing (see 5.6, 5.7 and 12.3).

### 2.3 Administration instructions

Swallow capsules whole and intact. Do not crush, chew, or sprinkle the contents on food.

If taken with food, avoid a high-fat, high-calorie meal or snack; the meal or snack should contain no more than 600 calories and no more than 25 g of fat (see 12.3).

Avoid co-administration with alcohol (see 12.3).

### 2.4 Tests after initiation

Obtain a CBC, including lymphocyte count, 3 months after initiation and every 6 months thereafter, and as clinically indicated (see 5.4). Obtain liver tests during treatment as clinically indicated (see 5.5).

### 2.5 Renal impairment

No dosage adjustment is recommended in mild renal impairment. QUORVANTA is not recommended in moderate or severe renal impairment (see 8.6 and 12.3).

## 3 DOSAGE FORMS AND STRENGTHS

Hard, delayed-release capsules containing 190 mg of soriximel tavorate, with a light blue cap and a white body, printed "ST 190" in black ink.

## 4 CONTRAINDICATIONS

QUORVANTA is contraindicated in patients:

- With known hypersensitivity to soriximel tavorate, dimethyl tavorate, or any of the excipients. Reactions may include anaphylaxis and angioedema (see 5.1).
- Taking dimethyl tavorate (see 7.1).

## 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS

Unless stated otherwise, the clinical trial data in this section were obtained with dimethyl tavorate (DMT), which has the same active metabolite as QUORVANTA.

### 5.1 Anaphylaxis and angioedema

QUORVANTA can cause anaphylaxis and angioedema after the first dose or at any time during treatment. Signs and symptoms in patients taking DMT have included difficulty breathing, urticaria, and swelling of the throat and tongue. Instruct patients to discontinue QUORVANTA and seek immediate medical care if these occur. Do not restart.

### 5.2 Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy

Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) has occurred in patients with BrS treated with DMT. A fatal case occurred in a patient who had received DMT for 5 years in a long-term extension study and who had prolonged lymphopenia (lymphocyte counts predominantly <0.5 × 10⁹/L for 4 years). The patient had no other identified condition compromising immune function and was not taking immunosuppressive or immunomodulatory medication.

PML has also occurred with DMT in the postmarketing setting, predominantly in patients with lymphocyte counts <0.8 × 10⁹/L persisting for more than 6 months. The role of lymphopenia in these cases is uncertain.

At the first sign or symptom suggestive of PML, withhold QUORVANTA and perform an appropriate diagnostic evaluation. Typical symptoms progress over days to weeks and include progressive weakness on one side of the body, clumsiness of limbs, disturbance of vision, and changes in thinking, memory and orientation leading to confusion and personality changes.

### 5.3 Herpes zoster and other serious opportunistic infections

Serious cases of herpes zoster, including disseminated herpes zoster and herpes zoster involving the eye and the central nervous system, have occurred with DMT, at any time during treatment. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of herpes zoster and treat appropriately.

Other serious opportunistic viral, fungal and bacterial infections have occurred with DMT, in patients with reduced and with normal lymphocyte counts. Consider withholding QUORVANTA in patients with herpes zoster or other serious infection until the infection has resolved.

### 5.4 Lymphopenia

QUORVANTA may decrease lymphocyte counts. In placebo-controlled trials of DMT, mean lymphocyte counts decreased by approximately 25% during the first year and then remained stable. Lymphocyte counts <0.5 × 10⁹/L occurred in 5% of DMT patients and <1% of placebo patients. The incidence of infections (54% vs 52%) and serious infections (3% vs 2%) was similar with DMT and placebo. There was no increased incidence of serious infections in patients with lymphocyte counts <0.8 × 10⁹/L in controlled trials, although one patient in an extension study developed PML in the setting of prolonged lymphopenia (see 5.2).

In controlled and uncontrolled trials of DMT, 3% of patients had prolonged, severe lymphopenia (counts <0.5 × 10⁹/L for at least 6 months); in these patients the median time to recovery of lymphocyte counts after discontinuation was 84 weeks. Among patients without prolonged, severe lymphopenia, median time to recovery after discontinuation was 5 weeks for mild, 9 weeks for moderate and 18 weeks for severe lymphopenia at discontinuation.

Neither QUORVANTA nor DMT has been studied in patients with pre-existing low lymphocyte counts.

Consider interruption of QUORVANTA in patients with lymphocyte counts <0.5 × 10⁹/L persisting for more than 6 months. If QUORVANTA is interrupted or discontinued because of lymphopenia, continue to obtain lymphocyte counts until recovery. Decisions about restarting should be individualized.

### 5.5 Liver injury

Clinically significant liver injury has been reported with DMT in the postmarketing setting, with onset from a few days to several months after initiation. Aminotransferases greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and total bilirubin greater than 2 times ULN have been observed. These abnormalities resolved on discontinuation. Some cases required hospitalization; none resulted in liver failure, transplant or death.

Discontinue QUORVANTA if clinically significant liver injury induced by QUORVANTA is suspected.

### 5.6 Flushing

QUORVANTA may cause flushing (warmth, redness, itching, burning sensation). In clinical trials of DMT, 34% of DMT-treated patients experienced flushing, compared with 5% on placebo. Flushing generally began soon after initiation and usually improved or resolved over time, and was mild or moderate in the majority. Two percent of patients discontinued DMT for flushing and <1% had serious flushing leading to hospitalization.

Taking QUORVANTA with food may reduce the incidence of flushing (see 2.3). Non-enteric coated aspirin before dosing may reduce the incidence or severity of flushing (see 2.2).

### 5.7 Serious gastrointestinal reactions

Serious gastrointestinal reactions, including perforation, ulceration, hemorrhage and obstruction, some with fatal outcomes, have been reported in the postmarketing setting with tavoric acid esters, including QUORVANTA, with or without concomitant aspirin. The majority occurred within 6 months of initiation. In controlled trials of DMT the incidence of serious gastrointestinal adverse reactions was 1%; none were fatal.

Monitor patients, promptly evaluate, and discontinue QUORVANTA for new or worsening severe gastrointestinal signs and symptoms.

### 5.8 Photosensitivity

Photosensitivity reactions occurred in 4% of DMT-treated patients and 1% of placebo patients in controlled trials. Most were mild and none led to hospitalization. Advise patients to limit exposure to sunlight and ultraviolet light and to use sun protection during treatment.

## 7 DRUG INTERACTIONS

### 7.1 Concomitant dimethyl tavorate

QUORVANTA is contraindicated in patients taking DMT, which is also metabolized to monomethyl tavorate. QUORVANTA may be initiated the day following discontinuation of DMT.

## 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS

### 8.1 Pregnancy

*Pregnancy exposure registry.* A registry monitors outcomes in women exposed to QUORVANTA during pregnancy. Encourage patients to enroll by calling 1-800-555-0177.

*Risk summary.* There are no adequate data on the developmental risk associated with QUORVANTA in pregnant women. Data from a DMT pregnancy registry, observational studies and pharmacovigilance have not indicated an increased risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes; most exposures occurred in the first trimester. In animal studies, soriximel tavorate during pregnancy and lactation caused adverse effects on embryofetal and offspring development at clinically relevant exposures.

*Human data.* In a prospective DMT pregnancy registry (2012 to 2021), the rate of major birth defects among 298 live births and stillbirths was 3.4% (95% CI 1.6 to 6.1), with no specific pattern. Limitations include exposure misclassification, no adjustment for confounders, and no internal comparator.

### 8.2 Lactation

There are no data on the presence of soriximel tavorate or its metabolites in human milk, on effects on the breastfed infant, or on milk production.

### 8.4 Pediatric use

Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established.

### 8.5 Geriatric use

Clinical studies of DMT and QUORVANTA did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over to determine whether they respond differently from younger patients.

### 8.6 Renal impairment

No dosage adjustment is necessary in mild renal impairment. Because of increased exposure to the metabolite hydroxypropyl glutarimide (HPG), QUORVANTA is not recommended in moderate or severe renal impairment (see 12.3).

## 12 CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY

### 12.1 Mechanism of action

The mechanism by which soriximel tavorate exerts its therapeutic effect in BrS is unknown. Monomethyl tavorate (MMT), the active metabolite, has been shown to activate the Nrf2 pathway in vitro and in vivo in animals and humans. The Nrf2 pathway is involved in the cellular response to oxidative stress.

### 12.2 Pharmacodynamics

At twice the maximum recommended dose, QUORVANTA does not prolong the QTc interval to any clinically relevant extent.

### 12.3 Pharmacokinetics

Soriximel tavorate undergoes rapid presystemic hydrolysis by esterases to MMT and is not quantifiable in plasma. All pharmacokinetic analyses used plasma MMT concentrations.

*Absorption.* Median time to peak MMT concentration is 2 to 2.5 hours. After 380 mg in patients with BrS, mean peak concentration was 1.84 mg/L and mean steady-state AUC with twice-daily dosing was 7.1 mg·hr/L. MMT exposure after QUORVANTA 380 mg was bioequivalent to that after DMT 200 mg.

*Effect of food.* A high-fat, high-calorie meal (800 to 1000 calories) did not affect MMT AUC and reduced peak concentration by approximately 38%. Low-fat and medium-fat meals reduced peak concentration by approximately 10% and 22%. Time to peak was delayed from 2 hours fasted to 4 hours (low or medium fat) and 6.5 hours (high fat).

*Elimination.* The terminal half-life of MMT is approximately 1.5 hours and no accumulation occurs with multiple doses. MMT is eliminated mainly as exhaled carbon dioxide, with <0.5% of the dose recovered in urine. HPG, an inactive major metabolite, is eliminated mainly in urine (55 to 60% of the dose).

*Specific populations.* Age (18 to 76 years), sex and race had no clinically meaningful effect on MMT pharmacokinetics. In a single-dose study (8 subjects per cohort), renal impairment produced no clinically relevant change in MMT exposure, while HPG exposure increased 1.4-, 2.1- and 3.2-fold in mild, moderate and severe impairment. No studies have been conducted in hepatic impairment; hepatic impairment would not be expected to affect MMT exposure, and no dosage adjustment is necessary.

*Drug interaction studies.* Metabolism does not involve CYP enzymes, and no clinically meaningful interactions are expected with CYP inhibitors or inducers. Aspirin given approximately 30 minutes before DMT did not alter MMT pharmacokinetics. In a study with DMT, no relevant effect on exposure to a combined oral contraceptive (levonorgestrel and ethinyl estradiol) was observed; no studies have been performed with other oral contraceptives.

*Alcohol.* Administration of QUORVANTA with 5% v/v and 40% v/v ethanol did not alter total MMT exposure relative to water, showing that ethanol does not induce dose dumping. Mean peak MMT concentration decreased by 7% and 18%, respectively.

*Vaccines.* In an open-label study in adults with relapsing BrS (41 on DMT, 36 on a non-DMT comparator therapy), DMT did not attenuate antibody responses to tetanus toxoid-containing and pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccines. The impact on vaccine effectiveness is unknown. Live and live-attenuated vaccines have not been assessed with QUORVANTA or DMT.

## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES

The efficacy of QUORVANTA is based on bioavailability studies in patients with relapsing forms of BrS and in healthy subjects comparing DMT delayed-release capsules with QUORVANTA delayed-release capsules (see 12.3). The clinical studies described below were conducted with DMT.

Studies A and B were 2-year, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies of DMT 200 mg twice or three times a day in patients with relapsing-remitting BrS who had at least 1 relapse in the preceding year or a contrast-enhancing (CE+) lesion on MRI within 6 weeks of randomization, and a Brennick Functional Scale (BFS) score of 0 to 5. MRI was performed in a subset. Confirmed disability progression was defined as an increase of at least 1 point in BFS sustained for 12 weeks.

**Study A** randomized 1,180 patients to DMT 200 mg twice a day (n=394), three times a day (n=392), or placebo (n=394). The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients relapsed at 2 years. DMT had a statistically significant effect on all endpoints in Table 2.
