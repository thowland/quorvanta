## QUORVANTA (kwor-VAN-tah) (soriximel tavorate) delayed-release capsules

Read this Patient Information before you start taking QUORVANTA and each time you get a refill. There may be new information. This information does not take the place of talking with your healthcare provider about your medical condition or your treatment.

### What is QUORVANTA?

QUORVANTA is a prescription medicine used to treat relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome (BrS) in adults, including a first episode of BrS, relapsing-remitting BrS, and active progressive BrS.

It is not known if QUORVANTA is safe and effective in children under 18 years of age.

### Who should not take QUORVANTA?

Do not take QUORVANTA if you:

- are allergic to soriximel tavorate, dimethyl tavorate, or any of the ingredients in QUORVANTA. See the end of this leaflet for a complete list of ingredients.
- are taking dimethyl tavorate.

### Before taking QUORVANTA, tell your healthcare provider about all of your medical conditions, including if you:

- have or have had low white blood cell counts or an infection
- have or have had liver problems
- have kidney problems
- have had a severe skin reaction to sunlight
- are pregnant or plan to become pregnant. It is not known if QUORVANTA will harm your unborn baby. If you become pregnant while taking QUORVANTA, talk to your healthcare provider about enrolling in the QUORVANTA Pregnancy Registry by calling 1-800-555-0177.
- are breastfeeding or plan to breastfeed. It is not known if QUORVANTA passes into your breast milk. Talk to your healthcare provider about the best way to feed your baby while taking QUORVANTA.

Tell your healthcare provider about all the medicines you take, including prescription and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and herbal supplements.

### How should I take QUORVANTA?

- Take QUORVANTA exactly as your healthcare provider tells you to take it.
- The starting dose is 1 capsule (190 mg) by mouth 2 times a day for 14 days.
- After 14 days, the usual dose is 2 capsules (380 mg) by mouth 2 times a day.
- Your healthcare provider may lower your dose to 1 capsule 2 times a day for a short time if you have side effects. Your dose will be increased again within 6 weeks.
- Swallow QUORVANTA capsules whole. Do not crush, chew, or sprinkle the capsule contents on food.
- QUORVANTA can be taken with or without food. Taking QUORVANTA with food may help reduce flushing.
- If you take QUORVANTA with food, avoid a high-fat, high-calorie meal or snack. The meal or snack should have no more than 600 calories and no more than 25 grams of fat.
- Do not drink alcohol at the time you take QUORVANTA.
- Protect your skin from the sun while taking QUORVANTA. See "What are the possible side effects of QUORVANTA?"
- If you take too much QUORVANTA, call your healthcare provider or go to the nearest hospital emergency room right away.

### What are the possible side effects of QUORVANTA?

QUORVANTA may cause serious side effects, including:

- **Allergic reactions (anaphylaxis and angioedema).** Stop taking QUORVANTA and get emergency medical help right away if you have trouble breathing, hives, or swelling of your face, lips, mouth, tongue, or throat.
- **PML (progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy).** PML is a rare brain infection that usually leads to death or severe disability. PML has happened in people taking dimethyl tavorate, a medicine that turns into the same active substance as QUORVANTA. Symptoms of PML get worse over days to weeks and can include weakness on one side of your body, clumsiness of your arms or legs, changes in your vision, and changes in your thinking, memory, or personality. Tell your healthcare provider right away if you have any of these symptoms.
- **Shingles (herpes zoster) and other serious infections.** Tell your healthcare provider right away if you have a painful rash with blisters, fever, chills, or other signs of infection.
- **Low white blood cell counts (lymphopenia).** QUORVANTA can lower the number of lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell that helps fight infection. Your healthcare provider will check your blood cell counts before you start QUORVANTA, 3 months after you start, and every 6 months after that.
- **Liver problems.** Your healthcare provider will do blood tests to check your liver before you start QUORVANTA and during treatment if needed. Tell your healthcare provider right away if you have severe tiredness, loss of appetite, pain on the right side of your stomach, dark urine, or yellowing of your skin or the whites of your eyes.
- **Serious stomach and bowel problems,** including tears, ulcers, bleeding, and blockages, which can be life-threatening. Tell your healthcare provider right away if you have severe stomach pain, vomiting, black or bloody stools, or you cannot pass stool or gas.
- **Skin reactions to sunlight.** QUORVANTA can make your skin more sensitive to sunlight. Limit your time in the sun, wear protective clothing, and use sunscreen.

**The most common side effects of QUORVANTA include:** flushing (redness, itching, or a rash), stomach pain, diarrhea, nausea, and headache. Flushing and stomach problems are most common when you first start QUORVANTA and usually get better over time. Taking QUORVANTA with food may help reduce flushing. Ask your healthcare provider whether taking aspirin before QUORVANTA might help with flushing.

These are not all the possible side effects of QUORVANTA. Call your doctor for medical advice about side effects. You may report side effects to Arden Quay Biosciences at 1-800-555-0142 or to FDA at 1-800-FDA-1088.

### How should I store QUORVANTA?

- Store QUORVANTA at room temperature between 68°F to 77°F (20°C to 25°C).
- Keep QUORVANTA in its original container with the lid tightly closed.
- Keep QUORVANTA and all medicines out of the reach of children.

### General information about the safe and effective use of QUORVANTA

Medicines are sometimes prescribed for purposes other than those listed in a Patient Information leaflet. Do not use QUORVANTA for a condition for which it was not prescribed. Do not give QUORVANTA to other people, even if they have the same symptoms that you have. It may harm them. You can ask your pharmacist or healthcare provider for information about QUORVANTA that is written for health professionals.
