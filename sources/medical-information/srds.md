# QUORVANTA Medical Information standard response documents (fictitious)

> Everything here is invented. These are reactive Medical Information letters for unsolicited requests; they are not promotional and the chatbot must not draw on them.

Standard disclaimer appended to every response: *The information provided is in response to your unsolicited request and is intended for healthcare professionals. It is not intended to be promotional. Please refer to the full Prescribing Information for QUORVANTA. Arden Quay Biosciences does not recommend the use of QUORVANTA in any manner other than as described in the Prescribing Information.*

## SRD-001 Use of QUORVANTA in pediatric patients
**Gap:** GAP-01 · **Scope:** off-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Can QUORVANTA be used in a 15-year-old with relapsing-remitting BrS? What dose?

The safety and effectiveness of QUORVANTA in patients under 18 years of age have not been established, and QUORVANTA is indicated for adults only. The pivotal studies of dimethyl tavorate (BRIGHTWATER and CASTLEREAGH) enrolled patients aged 18 to 55, and the QUORVANTA open-label studies enrolled adults only. Arden Quay Biosciences has no pediatric dosing recommendation and is not aware of controlled data in this population. A pediatric study of dimethyl tavorate was completed by another sponsor and is not part of the QUORVANTA labeling; we are unable to comment on it.

References: REF-001, REF-002, REF-003, REF-012

## SRD-002 Use of QUORVANTA in patients aged 65 and over
**Gap:** GAP-02 · **Scope:** beyond-label; limited data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Is any dose adjustment needed for a 71-year-old?

Clinical studies of dimethyl tavorate and QUORVANTA did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over to determine whether they respond differently from younger patients. No dosage adjustment based on age is described in the Prescribing Information. Age (18 to 76 years) had no clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of the active metabolite MMT. Renal function declines with age, and QUORVANTA is not recommended in moderate or severe renal impairment; an estimate of renal function is required before initiation.

References: REF-001, REF-004, REF-007

## SRD-003 Use of QUORVANTA in hepatic impairment
**Gap:** GAP-03 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** My patient has compensated cirrhosis. Can she take QUORVANTA?

No clinical studies of QUORVANTA have been conducted in patients with hepatic impairment. Because the active metabolite MMT is eliminated primarily as exhaled carbon dioxide through the tricarboxylic acid cycle and not through hepatic metabolism, hepatic impairment is not expected to affect MMT exposure, and the Prescribing Information states that no dosage adjustment is necessary. Separately, clinically significant liver injury has been reported with dimethyl tavorate in the postmarketing setting; liver tests are required before initiation and as clinically indicated during treatment, and QUORVANTA should be discontinued if clinically significant drug-induced liver injury is suspected. The decision to treat a patient with pre-existing liver disease rests with the treating physician.

References: REF-001, REF-004, REF-010

## SRD-004 Use of QUORVANTA in end-stage renal disease and dialysis
**Gap:** GAP-04 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Can QUORVANTA be given to a patient on hemodialysis?

QUORVANTA is not recommended in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment because of increased exposure to the inactive metabolite hydroxypropyl glavorimide (HPG), which rose 2.1-fold and 3.2-fold in moderate and severe impairment in a single-dose study. No patients on hemodialysis were studied, and the dialyzability of HPG is unknown. Arden Quay Biosciences has no data to support use in end-stage renal disease.

References: REF-001, REF-007

## SRD-005 Vaccination during QUORVANTA treatment
**Gap:** GAP-05 · **Scope:** beyond-label; partial data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Can my patient receive the shingles vaccine or a live vaccine while on QUORVANTA?

Live and live-attenuated vaccines have not been assessed with QUORVANTA or dimethyl tavorate, and the Prescribing Information makes no recommendation about them. In an open-label study, dimethyl tavorate did not attenuate antibody responses to tetanus toxoid-containing and pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccines; the impact on vaccine effectiveness is unknown. Whether a given vaccine is live or non-live, and its timing relative to treatment, should be determined by the treating physician with reference to current immunization guidance. Serious cases of herpes zoster have occurred with dimethyl tavorate.

References: REF-001, REF-014, REF-010

## SRD-006 QUORVANTA and breastfeeding
**Gap:** GAP-07 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Is QUORVANTA safe during breastfeeding?

There are no data on the presence of soriximel tavorate or its metabolites in human milk, on the effects on the breastfed infant, or on milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need for QUORVANTA and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant. Arden Quay Biosciences is not aware of published lactation data for dimethyl tavorate that would inform this question.

References: REF-001

## SRD-007 Comparative efficacy of QUORVANTA versus other disease-modifying therapies
**Gap:** GAP-09 · **Scope:** beyond-label; indirect data only · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** How does QUORVANTA compare with [other therapy] for relapse reduction?

No head-to-head trials of QUORVANTA or dimethyl tavorate against another disease-modifying therapy have been conducted by Arden Quay Biosciences. CASTLEREAGH included an open-label reference arm, but the study was not designed or powered for comparison with it and the Prescribing Information reports no comparative results. A published network meta-analysis (Rautenbach et al., 2026, preprint, not peer reviewed) ranked tavorate esters among seven therapies using indirect comparison; the authors caution that such comparisons across trials with different populations and eras are exploratory. Indirect comparisons are subject to confounding and should not be interpreted as evidence of superiority or equivalence.

References: REF-001, REF-003, REF-020

## SRD-008 Tolerability of QUORVANTA compared with dimethyl tavorate
**Gap:** GAP-10 · **Scope:** beyond-label; observational data only · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Is QUORVANTA easier on the stomach than dimethyl tavorate?

QUORVANTA and dimethyl tavorate share the same active metabolite, and the efficacy of QUORVANTA is based on bioequivalence. QUORVANTA was studied in approximately 620 patients in two open-label, uncontrolled studies, and its adverse reaction profile was consistent with the placebo-controlled experience with dimethyl tavorate; no comparison of tolerability was made. An interim, unadjusted analysis of insurance claims data presented as a congress poster (Dvořák-Mensah and Rautenbach, 2025) reported numerically lower discontinuation for gastrointestinal complaints with soriximel tavorate than with dimethyl tavorate at 6 months (11% versus 16%); this analysis is observational, interim and not peer reviewed, and Arden Quay Biosciences makes no claim of improved tolerability.

References: REF-001, REF-004, REF-012, REF-019

## SRD-009 Use of QUORVANTA in non-relapsing progressive Brennick syndrome
**Gap:** GAP-11 · **Scope:** off-label · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Would QUORVANTA help a patient with primary progressive BrS?

QUORVANTA is indicated for relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome and is not indicated for non-relapsing progressive disease. A single-center, uncontrolled series (Ibáñez-Corry and Dvořák-Mensah, 2024) reported no detectable change in BFS progression over 18 months in 28 patients with non-active progressive BrS treated off-label with a tavorate ester; the authors describe the study as underpowered. Arden Quay Biosciences does not recommend use outside the approved indication.

References: REF-001, REF-021

## SRD-010 Missed doses of QUORVANTA
**Gap:** GAP-13 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** What should a patient do if a dose is missed?

The Prescribing Information does not address missed doses, and Arden Quay Biosciences has no study data on the effect of a missed dose. The terminal half-life of the active metabolite MMT is approximately 1.5 hours and there is no accumulation with twice-daily dosing. Any guidance to a patient about a missed dose should come from the treating physician or pharmacist.

References: REF-001, REF-004

## SRD-011 Overdose with QUORVANTA
**Gap:** GAP-14 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** A patient took several days' supply at once. What is the management?

The Prescribing Information contains no overdosage section, and Arden Quay Biosciences has no clinical data on overdose. Management should be symptomatic and supportive, guided by a poison control center (1-800-222-1222 in the United States). Any overdose, whether or not associated with symptoms, should be reported to Arden Quay Biosciences Drug Safety at 1-800-555-0142.

## SRD-012 Combination of QUORVANTA with other disease-modifying therapies
**Gap:** GAP-15 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Can QUORVANTA be combined with another disease-modifying therapy?

QUORVANTA is contraindicated with dimethyl tavorate. The Prescribing Information contains no information on concomitant use with any other disease-modifying therapy, and the pivotal studies excluded such use. Arden Quay Biosciences has no data on combination regimens and does not recommend them. Because both QUORVANTA and many disease-modifying therapies can affect lymphocyte counts and infection risk, additive effects cannot be excluded.

References: REF-001, REF-002, REF-003

## SRD-013 Long-term efficacy of QUORVANTA
**Gap:** GAP-16 · **Scope:** beyond-label; uncontrolled data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Does the effect hold beyond 2 years?

The pivotal studies of dimethyl tavorate were 2 years in duration. In the integrated safety analysis (Halvorsen et al., 2022), which pooled controlled and uncontrolled follow-up of 2,740 patients for up to 11 years, annualized relapse rates in the open-label extension remained low through year 5 (0.14); the authors note the limitations of uncontrolled follow-up, including selective retention of patients who respond and tolerate treatment. The Prescribing Information does not include efficacy data beyond 2 years.

References: REF-001, REF-011

## SRD-014 Mechanism of action of QUORVANTA
**Gap:** GAP-17 · **Scope:** on-label · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** How does QUORVANTA work?

The mechanism by which soriximel tavorate exerts its therapeutic effect in Brennick syndrome is unknown. The active metabolite MMT has been shown to activate the nuclear factor (erythroid-derived 2)-like 2 (Nrf2) pathway, which is involved in the cellular response to oxidative stress, in vitro and in vivo in animals and humans; MMT is also a nicotinic acid receptor agonist in vitro. Whether these activities account for the clinical effect has not been established.

References: REF-001, REF-016

## SRD-015 Administration to patients who cannot swallow capsules
**Gap:** GAP-18 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Can the capsule be opened or given via feeding tube?

QUORVANTA capsules are delayed-release and must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed, chewed, or opened and sprinkled on food. Arden Quay Biosciences has no stability, pharmacokinetic or tolerability data on the capsule contents administered outside the intact capsule or through an enteral tube, and cannot recommend any such use.

References: REF-001

## SRD-016 QUORVANTA and hormonal contraception
**Gap:** GAP-06 · **Scope:** partial data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** Does QUORVANTA reduce the effectiveness of oral contraceptives?

In a study with dimethyl tavorate, no relevant effect on exposure to a combined oral contraceptive containing levonorgestrel and ethinyl estradiol was observed. No studies have been performed with oral contraceptives containing other progestogens, or with non-oral hormonal contraceptives. Metabolism of soriximel tavorate does not involve cytochrome P450 enzymes, and no clinically meaningful interactions with CYP substrates are expected.

References: REF-001, REF-018

## SRD-017 Initiating QUORVANTA in patients with low baseline lymphocyte counts
**Gap:** GAP-08 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** My patient's lymphocyte count is 0.7 before starting. Should I proceed?

Neither QUORVANTA nor dimethyl tavorate has been studied in patients with pre-existing low lymphocyte counts. A complete blood count including lymphocyte count is required before initiation. In the pooled analysis of dimethyl tavorate, mean lymphocyte counts fell by approximately 25% in the first year, and PML in the postmarketing setting occurred predominantly with counts below 0.8 × 10⁹/L persisting for more than 6 months. The decision to initiate treatment rests with the treating physician.

References: REF-001, REF-008, REF-010

## SRD-018 Switching to QUORVANTA from other disease-modifying therapies
**Gap:** GAP-22 · **Scope:** beyond-label; no data · **Approved:** 2026-09

**Typical question:** My patient is on a daily injectable. How long a washout do they need before starting QUORVANTA?

The Prescribing Information provides switching guidance only for dimethyl tavorate: QUORVANTA may be initiated the day following discontinuation of dimethyl tavorate, and co-administration is contraindicated. Switching from other disease-modifying therapies has not been studied, and Arden Quay Biosciences has no recommendation on washout periods or sequencing. The Prescribing Information of the previous therapy may describe the duration of its effects on the immune system. A complete blood count including lymphocyte count, liver tests and an estimate of renal function are required before initiating QUORVANTA regardless of prior therapy. The decision on timing rests with the treating physician.

References: REF-001
