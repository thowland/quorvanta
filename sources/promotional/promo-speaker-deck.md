### SD-01. QUORVANTA (soriximel tavorate) delayed-release capsules. For adults with relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome. `[IND-001]`

*SD-02. This program is sponsored by Arden Quay Biosciences, Inc. The speaker is paid by Arden Quay Biosciences to present on its behalf and presents only these approved slides. No continuing education credit is offered.*

**SD-03.** QUORVANTA is indicated for the treatment of relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome, to include first clinical episode, relapsing-remitting disease, and active progressive disease, in adults. `[IND-001]`

**SD-04.** The efficacy of QUORVANTA is based on bioavailability studies comparing dimethyl tavorate with QUORVANTA in patients with relapsing forms of Brennick syndrome and in healthy subjects. The clinical efficacy studies were conducted with dimethyl tavorate. `[EFF-001]`

**SD-05.** Studies A and B were 2-year, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies of dimethyl tavorate in patients with relapsing-remitting Brennick syndrome who had at least 1 relapse in the preceding year or a contrast-enhancing lesion on MRI within 6 weeks of randomization. `[EFF-002, EFF-001]`

**SD-06.** In Study A, 25% of patients treated with dimethyl tavorate 200 mg twice daily relapsed over 2 years, compared with 43% on placebo, a relative risk reduction of 46% (p<0.0001). This was the primary endpoint. In Study A, the annualized relapse rate was 0.18 with dimethyl tavorate 200 mg twice daily and 0.35 with placebo, a relative reduction of 49% (p<0.0001). `[EFF-003, EFF-004, EFF-001]`

**SD-07.** In Study B, the annualized relapse rate was 0.21 with dimethyl tavorate 200 mg twice daily and 0.39 with placebo, a relative reduction of 46% (p<0.0001). This was the primary endpoint. In Study B, 28% of patients treated with dimethyl tavorate 200 mg twice daily relapsed over 2 years, compared with 40% on placebo, a relative risk reduction of 33% (p=0.003). `[EFF-008, EFF-009, EFF-001]`

**SD-08.** In Study A, 15% of patients treated with dimethyl tavorate 200 mg twice daily had 12-week confirmed disability progression over 2 years, compared with 25% on placebo, a relative risk reduction of 41% (p=0.003). In Study B, there was no statistically significant effect of dimethyl tavorate on disability progression: 14% versus 18% with placebo (p=0.21). `[EFF-005, EFF-010, EFF-001]`

**SD-09.** In the Study A MRI cohort, the mean number of new or newly enlarging T2 lesions over 2 years was 3.1 with dimethyl tavorate and 15.8 with placebo (p<0.0001); 42% and 25% of subjects, respectively, had no new or newly enlarging lesions. In the Study A MRI cohort, the mean number of contrast-enhancing lesions at 2 years was 0.2 with dimethyl tavorate and 1.6 with placebo, a relative odds reduction of 86% (p<0.0001). In the Study B MRI cohort, the mean number of new or newly enlarging T2 lesions over 2 years was 4.8 with dimethyl tavorate and 16.2 with placebo, and the mean number of contrast-enhancing lesions at 2 years was 0.4 versus 1.9, a relative odds reduction of 72% (p<0.0001 for each). `[EFF-006, EFF-007, EFF-011, EFF-001]`

**SD-10.** Approximately 620 patients with relapsing-remitting Brennick syndrome were treated with QUORVANTA in clinical studies, approximately 410 of them for more than 1 year; the adverse reaction profile was consistent with the placebo-controlled experience with dimethyl tavorate. `[ADR-006]`

**SD-11.** QUORVANTA may cause flushing. In clinical trials of dimethyl tavorate, 34% of treated patients experienced flushing, compared with 5% on placebo. In trials of dimethyl tavorate, flushing generally began soon after initiation, usually improved or resolved over time, and was mild or moderate in the majority of patients. Taking QUORVANTA with food may reduce the incidence of flushing. If QUORVANTA is taken with food, a high-fat, high-calorie meal or snack should be avoided; the meal or snack should contain no more than 600 calories and no more than 25 g of fat. `[SAF-019, SAF-020, ADM-004, ADM-002]`

**SD-12.** In trials of dimethyl tavorate, gastrointestinal events were more frequent early in treatment, primarily in the first 2 months, and usually decreased over time; 5% of treated patients and 1% of placebo patients discontinued because of gastrointestinal events. `[ADR-004]`

**SD-13.** The starting dose of QUORVANTA is 190 mg twice a day, orally, for 14 days. After 14 days, the dose is increased to the maintenance dose of 380 mg, taken as two 190 mg capsules, twice a day. QUORVANTA capsules should be swallowed whole and intact, and should not be crushed, chewed, or sprinkled on food. `[DOS-001, DOS-002, ADM-001]`

**SD-14.** A complete blood count, including lymphocyte count, should be obtained before starting QUORVANTA. A complete blood count, including lymphocyte count, should be obtained 3 months after starting QUORVANTA and every 6 months thereafter, and as clinically indicated. `[MON-001, MON-003]`
